Languages, like biological species, diversify through time. As populations separate and interact with different neighbouring communities, linguistic features gradually change, leading to the emergence of distinct language varieties and, ultimately, new languages. Reconstructing these historical relationships is a central problem in historical linguistics, providing insight into the origins of language families, patterns of human migration, and the mechanisms through which linguistic change occurs. Since direct historical evidence is often incomplete or unavailable, researchers must instead infer these relationships from patterns of similarity and variation observed among contemporary and historical languages.
